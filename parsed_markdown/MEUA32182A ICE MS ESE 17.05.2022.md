Explanation

In S.I. engines detonation is caused by the auto-ignition of end gas towards the end of combustion whereas knocking in Cl engines is due to auto-ignition of the first charge at the start of combustion. Therefore, to avoid detonation in Sl engines auto ignition of end gas has to be prevented, and in Cl engines earlier possible auto ignition should occur.

The air fuel mixture that self ignites in Sl engines is homogeneous. Due to this rate of pressure rise and hence intensity of detonation is substantial compared to Cl engines, where there is no homogeneous air-fuel mixture present at the time of self-ignition.

Compression ratio must be limited in SI engines beyond which detonation would occur. But for Cl engines higher the compression ratio, lesser is the ignition delay and lesser probability of detonation (or diesel knock) occurring.

Larger size of cylinder promotes detonation in Sl engines whereas detonation in CIl engines is reduced with the same. For this reason, engines with cylinder diameters larger than 250 mm are almost always of CI type.

Fuels found good to prevent detonation in Sl engines have longer ignition delay time which proves to be poor for preventing knocking in Cl engines. This means fuels having higher octane rating have poor cetane rating and vice-versa.

# Emission Control Technologies

An increased diesel engine population has created pressures on controlling diesel PM and NOx emissions. The initial progress in diesel emission control was achieved through engine technologies, including changes in the combustion chamber design, improved fuel systems, charge air cooling, and special attention to lube oil consumption. Emission standards implemented in the 2005-2010 timeframe additionally require the use of exhaust aftertreatment methods on new diesel engines. These methods include diesel particulate filters, urea-SCR catalysts, and NOx adsorbers.
